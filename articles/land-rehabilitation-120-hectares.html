Land Rehabilitation Programme Reaches 120 Hectares Milestone

Shilungwa Mining and Resources has reached a significant environmental milestone, with its progressive land rehabilitation programme now having restored over 120 hectares of previously mined terrain across multiple project sites. The achievement reflects more than a decade of sustained investment in ecological restoration and demonstrates SMR's commitment to leaving a positive legacy in the landscapes where it operates.

The rehabilitation work encompasses a range of activities tailored to each site's unique conditions. At former open cast mining areas, SMR teams have reshaped landforms to approximate natural contours, replaced topsoil, and established drainage systems that prevent erosion and sedimentation. Native grass species, shrubs, and indigenous trees have been reintroduced through carefully managed planting programmes developed in consultation with local ecologists and environmental authorities.

Biodiversity recovery has been one of the programme's most encouraging outcomes. Monitoring surveys conducted over the past three years have recorded the return of numerous bird species, small mammals, and pollinator populations to rehabilitated sites. Wetland areas adjacent to former borrow pits have shown particularly strong recovery, with aquatic vegetation re-establishing naturally once water quality targets were met through SMR's pollution control measures.

A key component of the rehabilitation strategy has been the construction and maintenance of pollution control dams at active and legacy sites. These engineered structures capture and treat runoff water before it enters natural watercourses, ensuring that downstream ecosystems are protected from sediment loading and chemical contamination. SMR's expertise in pollution control dam construction has become a sought-after service, with several external clients commissioning similar infrastructure for their own operations.

Looking ahead, SMR has set a target of rehabilitating an additional 80 hectares over the next three years, with a particular focus on borrow pit restoration along major transport corridors. The company continues to refine its rehabilitation methodologies, incorporating lessons learned from each completed project and adopting new techniques in soil stabilisation and revegetation that accelerate ecological recovery timelines.

Powering the present, reshaping the future through responsible extraction and sustainable growth.
