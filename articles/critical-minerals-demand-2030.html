Global Demand for Critical Minerals Set to Double by 2030

The global appetite for critical minerals is accelerating at an unprecedented pace, with industry analysts projecting that demand for key resources such as lithium, manganese, and rare earth elements will more than double by the end of the decade. This surge is being driven primarily by the worldwide energy transition, as governments and industries invest heavily in renewable energy infrastructure, battery storage systems, and electric vehicle manufacturing.

Lithium remains at the centre of the conversation, with battery-grade lithium demand expected to grow fivefold by 2030. However, manganese — a mineral abundant across several African deposits — is emerging as an equally critical component in next-generation battery chemistries. Rare earth elements, essential for wind turbine magnets and EV motors, are also seeing supply constraints that have prompted major economies to diversify their sourcing strategies beyond traditional suppliers.

For the African mining sector, this represents a generational opportunity. The continent holds an estimated 30% of the world's critical mineral reserves, yet accounts for a fraction of global processing capacity. Countries such as South Africa, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Mozambique are positioning themselves as key players in the supply chain, attracting exploration investment and infrastructure development at levels not seen in decades.

Companies like Shilungwa Mining and Resources are well positioned to capitalise on this trend. With established expertise in exploration and mining services, material processing, and logistics, SMR offers the integrated capabilities that resource developers need to bring projects from discovery to production. The company's experience in open cast mining, material grading, and hauling provides a turnkey solution for clients seeking to fast-track mineral extraction in response to rising demand.

As the energy transition gathers momentum, the mining sector faces a dual challenge: scaling production to meet demand while maintaining the environmental and social standards that investors and regulators increasingly require. For service providers with deep operational experience and a commitment to responsible practices, the coming decade promises significant growth — and SMR intends to be at the forefront of that opportunity.
