Renewable Energy Integration Cuts Site Emissions by 22%

Shilungwa Mining and Resources has achieved a 22% reduction in carbon emissions across two of its operational sites following the deployment of solar photovoltaic arrays and battery energy storage systems. The milestone marks a significant step forward in the company's broader sustainability strategy and its commitment to reducing the environmental footprint of mining operations.

The solar installations, comprising over 2,400 panels across both sites, generate enough electricity to power processing equipment, site offices, and lighting systems during peak daylight hours. Paired with lithium-ion battery storage units, the system provides reliable power continuity during cloud cover and into the early evening, reducing dependence on diesel generators that have traditionally been the primary energy source at remote mining locations.

The financial impact has been equally compelling. Energy costs at the two pilot sites have decreased by approximately 18% since the renewable systems became fully operational, with further savings expected as battery technology improves and additional capacity is added. The reduction in diesel consumption has also lowered fuel logistics costs and reduced the frequency of hazardous fuel deliveries to site, contributing to both cost efficiency and improved safety outcomes.

The emissions reduction aligns with SMR's 2030 net-zero roadmap, which sets progressive targets for decarbonising operations across all project sites. The company is currently evaluating the feasibility of expanding solar installations to three additional locations and exploring hybrid wind-solar configurations for sites with favourable wind profiles. These efforts complement SMR's existing environmental management practices, including pollution control dam construction and progressive land rehabilitation.

For SMR, the transition to renewable energy is not simply an environmental obligation — it is a strategic investment that strengthens the company's competitive position. Mining clients and project partners are increasingly evaluating service providers on their sustainability credentials, and the ability to demonstrate measurable emissions reductions is becoming a differentiator in tender processes. By leading on clean energy adoption, SMR is building resilience into its business model while contributing to the broader decarbonisation of the mining sector.
